# Wind-assisted propulsion: planning and accounting

Engine 6.8.0 adds rigid wings, suction wings and other wind-assisted propulsion
systems as equipment that can be selected alongside fuel switching, shore power
and pooling. This is a performance-input model, not an aerodynamic or weather-
routing model. One reviewed wind package is evaluated per vessel.

## Set up a vessel

In the vessel editor, choose **Evaluate a wind retrofit** under **Wings &
wind-assisted propulsion**. Enter annual consumption **before** the proposed
wind savings. For every sea row, enter both:

- **Propulsion share:** the fraction of that row's fuel serving propulsion,
  excluding hotel loads, boilers and other unaffected consumers.
- **Net wind saving on propulsion:** the assessed reduction in that propulsion
  fuel at the same transport service and speed, after the wind equipment's
  auxiliary energy requirement. Use route/weather/season/load-specific evidence.

Use explicit zeros for unaffected rows, including pilot fuel that must remain.
For example, 1,000 tonnes, 80% propulsion and 10% net saving removes 80 tonnes,
leaving 920 tonnes before any fuel switching. The 200 tonnes of non-propulsion
fuel remain. The application does not infer propulsion shares or savings.

The net estimate must account for all WAPS auxiliary energy in the physical fuel
inputs. Separate additional auxiliary-fuel streams are not generated by this
model; installations needing a change in consumer/fuel mix require a separately
reconciled consumption assessment. A gross sail-performance percentage is not a
valid net-savings input. Savings must also remain valid for the nominated
alternative system and its entered efficiency/pilot requirements.

Enter full installation and downtime investment, annual maintenance and other
incremental operating costs, and the technical review reference. Do not include
auxiliary fuel cost again if its consumption is already included in net savings.
The retrofit is assumed available from the beginning of the case horizon:
capital is charged once in its first year, operating costs in every active year.
The search selects or rejects the whole installation; it does not prorate its
investment by fuel replacement share.

**Existing wings:** choose the existing-equipment option and enter consumption
that already includes their operational effect. Clear proposed savings (blank or
zero), set new wind investment to zero, and enter annual wind operating costs
once. All strategies retain the existing equipment and its annual cost. The
baseline is not reconstructed into a hypothetical vessel without wings.

**Imports:** existing Excel and CSV templates remain compatible. New imported
vessels start with no wind assumption or reward. Open each imported vessel to
review the wind inputs; those technical/evidence inputs are not inferred from
fleet consumption or imported as a certificate.

## Physical accounting and fuel substitution

For each voyage, source fuel and sea category:

    fuel_after_wind = original_tonnes × (1 − propulsion_share × net_saving)
    fuel_replaced = fuel_after_wind × selected_replacement_fraction
    fuel_retained = fuel_after_wind − fuel_replaced

Alternative-system thermal demand is the replaced energy multiplied by the
entered relative system energy requirement, split into alternative fuel and MGO
pilot. All quantities remain on the same voyage leg. EU berth and non-EU berth
receive no wind reduction; eligible EU berth can still use the separate OPS
choice. Physical tonnes drive purchase costs, supply caps and ETS accounting.
FuelEU annual source attribution is recalculated from the resulting inventory.

The switch optimizer retains weights and its aggregate replacement limit on the
original sea-demand basis. With wind, this is a conservative service-share cap:
selected fractions operate on the smaller remaining demand. Fuel-level summaries
show actual remaining/replaced tonnes, while saved voyage-leg fractions preserve
the exact selected decision. The audit reconciles each original leg as:

    original = net wind reduction + fuel replaced + current fuel retained

The wind choice is included in both the fuel-level seed search and the continuous
leg search, with an additional all-wind starting point. Fleet supply constraints,
banking and pooling are repriced for every candidate. The existing bounded search
does not guarantee a global optimum and still uses the full-fleet pooling model.

## Separate FuelEU reward

Regulation (EU) 2023/1805, Annex I, multiplies attained annual intensity by a wind
reward factor. Commission guidance, section 4.5, Table 4.12, describes discrete
bands (no rounding or interpolation):

| Reviewed Pwind / Pprop | Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Less than 0.05 | 1.00 |
| At least 0.05 and less than 0.10 | 0.99 |
| At least 0.10 and less than 0.15 | 0.97 |
| At least 0.15 | 0.95 |

Pwind is available effective wind power determined under MEPC.1/Circ.896. Pprop
uses the prescribed EEDI/EEXI propulsion-power definition, including applicable
shaft motors. Record the technical-file/recognised-body evidence separately from
the operational savings estimate, and elect the reward explicitly. The app does
not authenticate documents or grant installation/verifier approval. Prospective
retrofit results depend on completing that reviewed installation.

    attained_intensity = intensity_after_fuel/OPS/RFNBO_accounting × wind_factor
    compliance_balance = (target − attained_intensity) × covered_physical_MJ

The wind factor does not add wind energy to the denominator, change fuel factors,
double RFNBO energy, or reduce ETS emissions. A vessel outside FuelEU receives no
FuelEU reward, but can still have physical fuel savings. Unchecked reward means
factor 1.00 even when physical savings are selected. Existing wings apply their
reviewed elected factor in the reference as well as the other strategies.

For an independent example, use MGO 1,000 t intra-EU sea plus 40 t EU berth,
80% propulsion, 10% net saving and a reviewed power ratio of 0.10. Final MGO
before switching is 960 t, covered energy 40,992,000 MJ, and the 2026-onwards
ETS mass at the library defaults is `960 × (3.206 + 28×0.00005 + 265×0.00018)`.
FuelEU intensity uses the same MGO pathway intensity multiplied by 0.97;
the 0.97 is never applied to that ETS mass or physical energy.

## Reading the result

The report identifies retrofit selection versus retained existing equipment,
annual net current-fuel reduction before switching, wind cost, the intensity
before the wind reward, the multiplier and the resulting compliance balance.
The package comparison reconciles discounted fuel, ETS, penalties, pooling,
electricity and implementation differences to the total saving against the
reference. These combined effects must not be added to standalone technology
savings: fuel switching and pooling needs change when wind is selected.

CSV includes wind fields, evidence and leg reconciliation; JSON retains all inputs
and results. Historical reports are neither recalculated nor rewritten. Older
input records default to no wind; old CSV rows leave unavailable wind data blank.

## Boundaries and sources

Performance and decisions remain fixed over the horizon. Mid-year commissioning,
delayed installation, weather routing, sail sizing, changing engine efficiency or
methane slip at reduced loads, and class/structural/operational approvals require
separate assessment. Compare different reviewed performance assumptions as
separate cases. Current LNG defaults and RFNBO gates continue to apply.
