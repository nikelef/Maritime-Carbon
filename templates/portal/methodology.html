This application supports commercial planning. It does not certify fuel evidence, approve monitoring plans, arrange pools or submit statutory compliance reports.

FuelEU Maritime

Annual intensity and compliance balance are calculated for each ship above 5,000 GT. Positive balance is a surplus; negative balance is a deficit. A surplus becomes cash only through a separate commercial transaction, which this model does not assume.

Balance [tCO₂e] = (target − attained intensity) × scoped energy [MJ] ÷ 1,000,000

Residual deficits use Annex IV’s €2,400 per equivalent tonne of fuel, divided by attained intensity × 41,000 MJ/t. Consecutive penalty years increase that penalty by 10 percentage points for each additional year.

EU Emissions Trading System

For ordinary cargo/passenger ships of at least 5,000 GT, the model covers all emissions on intra-EU voyages and in EU ports, and half of each fuel’s emissions on EU–non-EU voyages.

Reporting year 2025 uses 70% of scoped CO₂ emissions. Reporting years from 2026 use 100% and include CH₄ and N₂O with GWP100 values of 28 and 265.

ETS cost = scoped tonnes CO₂e × phase-in share × assumed EUA price

Eligible renewable fuels can have their CO₂ component zero-rated. Methane and nitrous oxide are retained from 2026. Selecting an eligibility checkbox is the user’s recorded assertion, not verification by the application.

Reporting year ≠ payment year

Costs are attributed to the emissions reporting year. ETS surrender is generally due by 30 September in the following year. FuelEU settlement likewise follows the reporting period. The NPV is a planning-year comparison, not a treasury payment schedule.

What the strategy search evaluates

Four policies are compared: current fuels with residual penalties, current fuels with pooling, compatible alternative fuel and shore-power choices, and those choices with pooling. Replacement is selected separately by voyage, current fuel and sea-leg category. A fuel-level search with a 2% total-sea-energy grid supplies a feasible starting result. Leg and transfer searches sample each feasible range in 2% intervals before continuous refinement. A bounded continuous search then adjusts individual legs and transfers replacement energy between legs, checking compliance and fuel-supply boundaries and refining quantities between sampled points. It uses two starts and up to three fleet passes. The overall ship limit is shared across fuels and legs. Decisions remain fixed throughout the horizon. The result is the lowest evaluated cost; the search does not prove a global optimum.

The consumption table records annual tonnes for each fuel and voyage/berth category, including current pilot fuel. Ordinary scope is 100% for intra-EU sea and EU berth, 50% for EU/non-EU sea, and 0% for other sea and non-EU berth. All categories contribute to fuel purchase costs and annual supply limits. Each voyage/fuel/sea-leg combination can have a different replacement fraction. Alternative fuel and MGO pilot energy are assigned to the same leg where current fuel is reduced. Their regulatory coverage follows that leg. The report gives each fuel’s reduction and retained tonnes, and the alternative tonnes required. The report compares equal first steps across current source fuels using full-horizon fleet present cost with other ships and OPS fixed, using proportional allocation as an illustrative comparison. The selected strategy remarks specify the final voyage and leg quantities. The complete mix is evaluated together. Berth fuel is unchanged by sea-fuel switching. Shore power replaces only the specified share of EU berth fuel; boiler and other non-replaceable use must be excluded. One generator efficiency is applied across the replaced EU berth fuels.

Fuel replacement preserves service demand using the entered relative energy requirement; thermal input is conserved only at a ratio of 100%. The alternative system's energy includes its MGO pilot. Full conversion/downtime investment and incremental annual costs are charged when a switch is selected. Shore electricity is calculated from replaced generator output, using the entered generator and connection efficiencies. Electricity cost, initial OPS investment and annual operating costs are included. Shore electricity has zero emissions for the FuelEU intensity calculation under Annex I; this is not a claim of zero lifecycle grid emissions.

Banking and pooling policy

Opening banks belong to individual ships and first cover that ship’s deficit. Current surplus is carried forward unless contributed to a modelled fleet pool. Banked surplus is never sold or transferred to another ship. A pooled result is modelled only if the full included fleet can be settled with available current surplus and, where enabled, external capacity. Internal pools retain a positive buffer. External counterparties must retain a positive overall pool balance and meet Article 21 requirements; this is a condition of the estimate, not a verified fact. If capacity is insufficient, the model falls back to individual settlement and explains residual penalties.

Borrowing is not selected: it involves a ship-specific limit, 10% next-year repayment uplift, a ban on consecutive borrowing and restrictions on pooling. The ledger provides the numerical borrowing ceiling only. No terminal bank value, surplus sales, fuel availability expansion or automatic future rule changes are assumed.

RFNBO pathways & evidence

RFNBO options include e-LNG, e-methanol, e-ammonia, hydrogen fuel cells, hydrogen combustion and e-diesel. These are distinct production/consumer pathways, not automatically eligible because of their chemical names. They require certified FuelEU-adjusted WtT; physical combustion emissions are added by the engine. Unknown methanol/ammonia and hydrogen-combustion factors must be supplied with measured-factor evidence before the pathway is enabled.

For eligible RFNBO fuel with the elective reward enabled, its contribution to the GHG-intensity denominator is doubled through 2033, reverting to 1× from 2034. The physical energy used for compliance balance is not doubled. The reward election is a fixed fuel assumption, not an optimization variable. ETS CO₂ zero-rating is reviewed separately, and it does not erase methane or N₂O.

Conditional 2034 subtarget

The potential 2% RFNBO requirement is conditional on monitoring and Commission assessments. The default shows potential shortfall without pricing a penalty. An explicitly assumed-active stress test uses the positive shortfall in MJ, divided by 41,000 and multiplied by the user's assumed Pd, with consecutive-penalty uplift. Pd is a fixed case-wide price-difference assumption in EUR per equivalent tonne, not an official future tariff. Assumed-inactive cases also retain a warning.

OPS is excluded from this subtarget's energy basis. No separate RFNBO pooling, banking or equivalent-fuel exemption is assumed. GHG-intensity bank/pool balances cannot settle a separate RFNBO energy shortfall. Penalty totals include any assumed RFNBO charge, with both components separately available in exports.

Supported use and material limits

The customer workflow covers oil, eligible non-food/feed HVO/FAME, LNG, bio-LNG and eligible RFNBO pathways for conventional cargo/tanker, container and passenger ships. It uses ordinary EU voyage allocation shared by both regimes. Ship-specific EEA application dates, small-island/outermost-region derogations, excluded port calls, transhipment ports, ice-class adjustments and other exemptions must be assessed separately. A scope confirmation is required to run a case.

LNG, bio-LNG and e-LNG use the selected consumer's default slip: 3.1% Otto medium speed, 1.7% Otto slow speed, 0.2% Diesel slow speed or 2.6% LBSI. Combusted CO₂ and N₂O are reduced for the slipped fraction; methane is retained. Separate sea, berth and alternative methane consumer classes are modelled. Mixed engines, measured/load-dependent slip, additional venting and non-MGO pilot fuel for the alternative system need separate assessment. Mandatory OPS port-call penalties and exemptions for container/passenger ships are also outside the cost model. Consequently, the model cannot establish the full legal compliance cost for cases affected by those rules. HSFO requires a separately confirmed sulphur-compliance arrangement.

Prices and fuel properties are editable planning assumptions, not current market quotations or universal regulatory factors. Actual supplier batches, evidence validity, engine factors, operational fuel standards, fuel availability, costs under charter parties, taxes, financing, hedging and other regulations may change the commercial answer. Record additional common costs in the case assumptions and use the downloadable audit file for independent review.
