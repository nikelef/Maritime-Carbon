Wings & wind-assisted propulsion

Rigid wings, suction wings, rotor sails, kites and other wind-assisted propulsion systems (WAPS) are represented by one reviewed equipment package per vessel. Wind can combine with fuel switching, shore power and pooling. The app uses your assessed performance; it does not predict aerodynamic output or choose a sail design.

How to apply wings in a case

  1. Open the vessel and expand Wings & wind-assisted propulsion. Choose a proposed retrofit or existing equipment.
  2. For a retrofit, enter consumption before wind savings. In Consumption & voyage mix, enter a reviewed propulsion share and net propulsion-fuel saving for every sea row. Use explicit zero savings for unaffected rows, including pilot fuel that must remain.
  3. Include the equipment's auxiliary demand in the net saving at the same speed and transport service. Enter full installation/downtime cost, annual maintenance and operating cost, and the technical review reference.
  4. Elect the separate FuelEU wind reward only with reviewed Pwind/Pprop data and its own evidence reference. Run Compare strategies to see whether the whole retrofit is selected at the entered performance and costs.

Physical fuel saving comes first

Fuel after wind [t] = original sea fuel [t] × (1 − propulsion share × net saving)

For example, 1,000 t of sea fuel with 80% propulsion share and 10% net saving removes 80 t, leaving 920 t before fuel switching. Berth fuel and non-propulsion demand are unchanged. Savings must remain valid for the nominated alternative system, including its efficiency and pilot requirements.

Reduced physical consumption changes fuel purchase cost and applicable ETS emissions. FuelEU allocation is recalculated from the remaining inventory. Alternative-fuel replacement then operates on that remaining demand, on the same voyage leg. The switch cap retains the original sea-demand weights, conservatively limiting the fractions applied after wind.

Existing wings and investment timing

For existing wings, enter consumption already net of their effect; clear proposed savings and set new wind investment to zero. The equipment, annual operating cost and any reviewed elected reward remain in every strategy, including the reference. There is no second fuel reduction or hypothetical reconstruction of a ship without wings.

A proposed retrofit starts at the beginning of the horizon if selected. Full installation and downtime investment is charged once; annual operating costs recur throughout the horizon. Auxiliary fuel already included in net savings must not be charged again. Delayed commissioning and changing annual performance require separate cases.

Separate FuelEU wind reward

Pwind is available effective wind power assessed under MEPC.1/Circ.896. Pprop uses the prescribed EEDI/EEXI propulsion-power definition, including applicable shaft motors. These design-power values do not supply the operational fuel-saving percentage. Apply the discrete bands without rounding or interpolation:

Read the whole package

What the package changes in the report reconciles fuel, ETS, FuelEU penalties, pooling, electricity and implementation costs against the reference. These interactions cannot be added to standalone technology savings. Each leg reconciles as original fuel = wind reduction + switched fuel + retained fuel. The annual ledger shows intensity before reward, multiplier and resulting balance.

Weather routing, sail sizing, structural/class approval, changing engine efficiency and load-dependent methane slip remain outside this model. Imported vessels start with no wind assumption; review the wind inputs after import. Historical reports keep their original results.

What the strategy search evaluates

The question is: which tested combination gives the lowest total modelled cost for your fleet over the selected years? The search compares fuel replacement, shore power, proposed wind equipment and pooling under your entered prices and limits. It does not simply choose the cheapest fuel or the lowest emissions.

1. What cost is minimized?

Annual fleet cost = fuel + electricity + EU ETS + FuelEU penalties + external pooling + implementation + other entered costs
Present cost = first-year cost + second-year cost ÷ (1 + r) + third-year cost ÷ (1 + r)² + …

r is your annual discount rate (7% means r = 0.07). The first year is undiscounted. Implementation includes selected fuel-conversion, shore-power and wind costs: capital is charged once at the start, with entered annual costs thereafter. Fuel and EUA prices follow the escalation assumptions explained under Costs, prices & present-value comparison.

For a simple one-year example, if a change adds €10,000 to fuel cost but saves €14,000 in ETS and FuelEU penalties, with everything else unchanged:

Change in total cost = +€10,000 − €14,000 = −€4,000 → €4,000 saving

The actual calculation also includes changes in electricity, investment, pooling and future-year balances. It evaluates their combined effect across the fleet.

2. Which strategies are compared?

Four policies: permitted choices, not actions that must be used
PolicyWhat can change?
Current fuelsKeep the entered consumption; use the ship's bank under the model's policy and price remaining deficits as penalties.
Current fuels + poolingThe same physical plan, with feasible internal pooling and, if enabled, external surplus purchases.
Fuel & equipment choicesAllow compatible alternative-fuel replacement, shore power and a proposed wind retrofit, without pooling.
Combined choices + poolingThe same fuel/equipment search, with pooling allowed.

Existing wings and their entered annual cost remain in every policy. Proposed retrofits are considered only in the last two. A permitted choice can remain unused: a pooling-enabled policy may buy no surplus, and a proposed retrofit may be rejected. Banking and pooling follow the model's stated settlement policy; their rules are not freely optimized.

3. What does the search choose?

  • Fuel replacement: a separate replacement fraction for each voyage, current fuel and sea-leg category. The alternative fuel/system is the one you nominated for that vessel; the search does not select another fuel from the whole library.
  • Shore power: off or on, when readiness is confirmed. If on, it uses your entered EU-berth replacement shares and efficiencies.
  • Proposed wind: do not install or install the entire reviewed package. Its performance and costs are your inputs. Existing wings are already part of the starting consumption.
Current fuel replaced on a sea row = fuel remaining after selected wind savings × that row's replacement fraction

The replacement supplies the same voyage leg using the entered energy requirement, split between the nominated alternative and any MGO pilot. FuelEU annual energy attribution is recalculated separately; it does not move physical consumption or its ETS scope between legs. Sea-fuel switching does not replace berth fuel.

4. Which limits must be respected?

Each replacement fraction lies between 0% and 100%. The ship's overall switch limit is shared across its sea rows, using their original energy shares:

Overall switch share = Σ (row's original sea-energy share × row's replacement fraction) ≤ vessel's switch limit

Σ means “add the contribution from every sea row”. If two rows each represent 50% of original sea energy, replacing 40% of one and 0% of the other uses 50% × 40% + 50% × 0% = 20% overall. The limit is not applied independently to each row. With wind, these weights remain on the original demand; the selected fractions act on the smaller remaining fuel demand.

Annual purchases of each fuel across all ships ≤ entered annual supply cap, where specified

Fuel eligibility, technical compatibility, required pilot fuel, equipment readiness and available pool capacity also apply. A candidate within these model limits can still have a residual FuelEU deficit: its penalty is included in cost. The search does not require every candidate to have zero penalties or establish full statutory compliance.

5. How does it find a cheaper combination?

  1. Find a starting plan: make a useful first comparison.

    The first search groups current sea consumption by fuel, before fine-tuning individual voyage legs. It tries broad replacement amounts, including no replacement, and permitted shore-power/wind choices. Each candidate is priced for the whole fleet over the full horizon. The cheapest feasible starting result is carried into the more detailed leg search.

    What does the initial 2% mean? For the fuel-level allocation adjustments, a normal step is 2% of the ship's original total sea energy. This is an energy amount, not 2% of the current fuel price, fuel tonnes or remaining deficit.

    Normal initial allocation step [MJ] = 0.02 × original total sea energy [MJ]

    For example, with 100,000 MJ of original sea energy, one step is 2,000 MJ. In a simple single-current-fuel example with a 7.5% switch limit, trial shares include 0%, 2%, 4%, 6% and exactly 7.5%. The last trial is 7,500 MJ, even though it is not a multiple of 2,000 MJ. The search also respects how much of a particular fuel is available and the remaining ship limit.

    “Coarse” describes this initial resolution. It does not round the final answer to 2% or require the selected plan to use alternative fuel.

  2. Try changes: improve where replacement and equipment are used.

    The next search separates each ship's consumption into voyage × current fuel × sea category. For example, Voyage 1 / MGO / intra-EU sea and Voyage 1 / MGO / EU–non-EU sea can receive different replacement fractions.

    It adjusts one ship at a time. The other ships' physical choices are temporarily held fixed, but the entire fleet cost is recalculated: changing one ship can change shared fuel availability, banking and pooling needs. Other ships are reconsidered as the search proceeds.

    Where both options are available, it tests shore power off/on together with a proposed wind retrofit off/on: neither, shore power only, wind only, or both. It uses your entered berth shares, efficiencies, wind performance and full equipment costs; it does not invent a new equipment size or fuel pathway.

    Why move replacement between sea rows? A ship may already be using its full switch limit, but using the alternative on a different row may cost less. Suppose the 20% overall allowance is split into 10 percentage points on row A and 10 on row B. Where row capacity allows, the search can try 5 points on A and 15 on B:

    Before: 10% + 10% = 20% of original sea energy
    Trial: 5% + 15% = 20% of original sea energy

    These are contributions to the ship-wide energy allowance, not the two rows' own replacement percentages. The trial keeps the total at 20% and changes where replacement happens. It does not move physical fuel between voyages. The alternative and any pilot fuel still supply the same leg on which current fuel is reduced.

  3. Check useful points: inspect the permitted range and important thresholds.

    For each individual-row adjustment or transfer, the search first finds the range allowed by the vessel's remaining switch allowance, each row's available fuel and shared annual supply caps. It then divides that feasible range into 50 equal intervals—51 sample points for a non-zero range when both ends are counted.

    Trial fraction = lower limit + (upper limit − lower limit) × k ÷ 50
    k = 0, 1, 2, …, 50

    This 2% is a different measure: it is 2% of the current adjustment range. If a row's replacement fraction can range from 0% to 20%, the interval is (20% − 0%) ÷ 50 = 0.4 percentage points. Samples are 0%, 0.4%, 0.8%, …, 20%, not 0%, 2%, 4%, …, 20%.

    The search adds relevant points beyond the regular samples:

    • Endpoints and supply limits: no change, maximum permitted change, and the point where a shared fuel-supply limit is reached.
    • FuelEU allocation changes: a source's eligible annual energy is fully used and another source starts filling the covered energy total, or the RFNBO reward changes the preferred source order. These are accounting changes, not movements of physical fuel.
    • Compliance and settlement thresholds: candidate points where a ship's deficit disappears, accumulated balances or opening banks become sufficient, or fleet/pool capacity may be enough to cover the remaining deficit. Full simulation checks the actual settlement and cost under the model's rules.

    For example, a deficit might disappear at 6.7% replacement. The search looks for that threshold between samples, rather than testing only nearby grid values. It still checks other quantities: eliminating the deficit is not automatically the cheapest overall plan.

    Why check both sides of 2033/2034? The model's elected RFNBO denominator reward is 2× through 2033 and 1× from 2034. The same physical fuel mix can therefore give a different FuelEU intensity and different allocation thresholds. If the horizon spans the change, the search checks both regimes and prices every year. The physical replacement/equipment decisions remain fixed across those years.

    The 50 intervals apply to each adjustment range, not to the whole case. The total evaluation count depends on the inputs and can be much higher than 50 after threshold checks and refinement.

  4. Refine between the samples. Search around the best sampled point for a cheaper quantity. Final replacement amounts are not restricted to 2% multiples.
  5. Recalculate and repeat. Score each candidate using the full fleet and all selected years, including bank carry-forward, settlement and penalty history. The leg search tries the fuel-search result and the no-new-action plan, plus an all-proposed-wind starting plan when applicable. Each start gets up to three fleet passes, stopping earlier if no improvement is found.
Keep an improvement when new fleet present cost < current fleet present cost, beyond numerical tolerance

A supply-infeasible candidate is rejected. The search retains its feasible starting result unless it finds a cheaper plan. Selected physical decisions stay fixed throughout the horizon; it does not choose a new fuel mix each year or schedule a later installation.

6. How should you read the answer?

Reported saving = current-fuels reference present cost − selected strategy present cost

The result is the lowest evaluated cost. This bounded search does not test every possible combination or prove a global minimum. Banking, pooling and fixed investment costs can make the cost pattern uneven.

Read the selected strategy's actual actions and voyage-leg quantities together. The report's first-step fuel comparison tests equal initial energy steps with other ships, the selected wind and shore-power choices fixed, using proportional leg allocation for illustration. It explains the cost trade-off; it is not an instruction to apply technologies one by one or add their standalone savings.

The price-sensitivity panel reprices saved decisions; it does not rerun the full optimization. Create a new run to optimize after changing fuel prices, technical performance or other assumptions.

FuelEU Maritime

Annual intensity and compliance balance are calculated for each ship above 5,000 GT. FuelEU uses GWP100 values of 25 for CH₄ and 298 for N₂O (RED Annex V, Part C(4)); ETS uses 28 and 265. These factors apply to derived methane/RFNBO intensities and the new-case oil defaults. Saved reports preserve their recorded factors. New comparisons reject older fossil oil overrides until the case's supported defaults are restored. Positive balance is a surplus; negative balance is a deficit. A surplus becomes cash only through a separate commercial transaction, which this model does not assume.

Balance [tCO₂e] = (target − attained intensity) × scoped energy [MJ] ÷ 1,000,000

Residual deficits use Annex IV’s €2,400 per equivalent tonne of fuel, divided by attained intensity × 41,000 MJ/t. Consecutive penalty years increase that penalty by 10 percentage points for each additional year.

EU Emissions Trading System

For ordinary cargo/passenger ships of at least 5,000 GT, the model covers all emissions on intra-EU voyages and in EU ports, and half of each fuel’s emissions on EU–non-EU voyages.

Reporting year 2025 uses 70% of scoped CO₂ emissions. Reporting years from 2026 use 100% and include CH₄ and N₂O with GWP100 values of 28 and 265.

ETS cost = scoped tonnes CO₂e × phase-in share × assumed EUA price

Eligible renewable fuels can have their CO₂ component zero-rated. Methane and nitrous oxide are retained from 2026. Selecting an eligibility checkbox is the user’s recorded assertion, not verification by the application.

Reporting year ≠ payment year

Costs are attributed to the emissions reporting year. ETS surrender is generally due by 30 September in the following year. FuelEU settlement likewise follows the reporting period. The NPV is a planning-year comparison, not a treasury payment schedule.

Voyage scope & annual FuelEU allocation

The consumption table records annual tonnes for each fuel and voyage/berth category, including current pilot fuel. Ordinary scope is 100% for intra-EU sea and EU berth, 50% for EU/non-EU sea, and 0% for other sea and non-EU berth. All categories contribute to fuel purchase costs and annual supply limits. Each voyage/fuel/sea-leg combination can have a different replacement fraction. Alternative fuel and MGO pilot energy are assigned to the same leg where current fuel is reduced. Their ETS coverage follows that leg.

FuelEU accounting separately fills the ship’s annual covered energy total using its eligible energy sources to minimize attained intensity including the elected RFNBO reward, with conventional fuels, alternative fuel and pilot. Full consumption on intra-EU and EU–non-EU sea legs and EU berth is available for this attribution; the 50% factor sets the covered energy total, not a separate limit on each fuel. Shore electricity is included at zero intensity. Attribution stops when the covered physical energy total is filled, including a partial final source. It never increases consumption or changes fuel costs. Allocation minimizes total emissions divided by physical covered energy plus the elected extra RFNBO energy. With no active reward, sources fill in lowest-WtW order. With the reward, each source is ranked by WtW minus attained intensity times (reward − 1), iterating to the minimum ratio. Sorting only by WtW or by WtW divided by reward is insufficient. Equal scores use physical WtW, then fuel code and consumer order. Conventional fuels retain their internal lowest-WtW priority. Outside-scope sea and non-EU berth are excluded from the source inventory; MRV-reported FuelEU-exempt activity requires separate assessment. This app elects the permitted prioritized method described in Commission FuelEU guidance, section 4.4, pages 51–52; the method is not compulsory under the regulation.

Fuel eligibility, switching & supply limits

The fuel library covers conventional oil, eligible non-food/feed HVO/FAME, LNG, bio-LNG and the supported RFNBO pathways. Prices and permitted renewable-pathway factors are case inputs. Supported fossil oil calorific values and emission factors are fixed. A chemical name alone never establishes renewable eligibility.

HVO/FAME use an evidenced complete well-to-wake (WtW) intensity. Methane and RFNBO pathways use the pathway's well-to-tank (WtT) input plus the engine/consumer's tank-to-wake factors. Fossil LNG and the supported HSFO, VLSFO residual-oil assumption and MGO pathways retain their specified default factors. Fossil WtT and combustion CO₂ cannot be replaced by supplier values under FuelEU Article 10(4–5). This version also fixes fossil oil CH₄/N₂O and LCV; actual-factor or other oil-grade pathways need separate assessment. Unknown methanol, ammonia and hydrogen-combustion factors require measured-factor evidence; missing factors are not treated as zero.

FuelEU eligibility and ETS CO₂ zero-rating are separate assertions. Review both and record their supporting evidence. Methane and nitrous oxide are not erased by renewable CO₂ zero-rating. The app neither authenticates certificates nor confirms sustainability, origin, sulphur or engine-manufacturer approval.

Each vessel nominates one alternative fuel/system. Its technical compatibility, overall alternative-energy limit, relative energy requirement, MGO pilot share and full conversion/downtime costs constrain the evaluated switch. A percentage energy limit is not a universal Bxx mass/volume blend approval. Annual fuel supply caps apply across the fleet to all purchased consumption, including outside-scope use and pilot fuel; a baseline exceeding a cap must be corrected before comparison.

Fuel replacement preserves service demand using the entered relative energy requirement; thermal input is conserved only at a ratio of 100%. The alternative system's energy includes its MGO pilot. Full conversion/downtime investment and incremental annual costs are charged when a switch is selected.

Shore power (OPS)

Berth fuel is unchanged by sea-fuel switching. Shore power replaces only the specified share of EU berth fuel; boiler and other non-replaceable use must be excluded. One generator efficiency is applied across the replaced EU berth fuels.

Shore electricity is calculated from replaced generator output, using the entered generator and connection efficiencies. Electricity cost, initial OPS investment and annual operating costs are included. Shore electricity has zero emissions for the FuelEU intensity calculation under Annex I; this is not a claim of zero lifecycle grid emissions.

Shore electricity [MWh] = replaced EU-berth fuel [t] × LCV [MJ/t] × generator efficiency ÷ connection efficiency ÷ 3,600

Set readiness, the default replaceable berth share and any EU-berth row override. The optimizer selects or rejects shore power for a vessel; the entered replacement shares then apply. Sea and non-EU berth consumption are unaffected by this choice. Include installation/downtime cost even where fuel savings are attractive; use zero new capital only when the connection is already available.

Scope limit: the app evaluates optional energy replacement. It does not check individual port-call OPS obligations, connection availability, exemptions or non-connection penalties for container/passenger ships. A low modelled cost or zero GHG-intensity penalty does not establish compliance with those separate obligations.

RFNBO pathways & evidence

RFNBO options include e-LNG, e-methanol, e-ammonia, hydrogen fuel cells, hydrogen combustion and e-diesel. These are distinct production/consumer pathways, not automatically eligible because of their chemical names. They require certified FuelEU-adjusted WtT; physical combustion emissions are added by the engine. Unknown methanol/ammonia and hydrogen-combustion factors must be supplied with measured-factor evidence before the pathway is enabled.

For eligible RFNBO fuel with the elective reward enabled, its contribution to the GHG-intensity denominator is doubled through 2033, reverting to 1× from 2034. The physical energy used for compliance balance is not doubled. The reward election is a fixed fuel assumption; the annual allocation optimizes its use. For 50 MJ covered, with 40 MJ biofuel at WtW 20, 20 MJ RFNBO at 25 and 40 MJ fossil at 90 available, attribution is 30 MJ biofuel plus 20 MJ RFNBO: (30×20 + 20×25)/(50+20) = 15.714 gCO₂e/MJ. Physical-WtW ordering would give 17.5. This example illustrates the accounting optimum; it does not prove a global fleet-cost optimum. ETS CO₂ zero-rating is reviewed separately, and it does not erase methane or N₂O.

Conditional 2034 subtarget

The potential 2% RFNBO requirement is conditional on monitoring and Commission assessments. The default shows potential shortfall without pricing a penalty. An explicitly assumed-active stress test uses the positive shortfall in MJ, divided by 41,000 and multiplied by the user's assumed Pd, with consecutive-penalty uplift. Pd is a fixed case-wide price-difference assumption in EUR per equivalent tonne, not an official future tariff. Assumed-inactive cases also retain a warning.

The subtarget uses physical fuel and eligible RFNBO consumption independently of GHG-intensity source allocation: 100% intra-EU/EU berth, 50% EU/non-EU sea, and 0% outside-scope/non-EU berth. Actual OPS electricity is excluded even if negative-intensity fuel displaces OPS in GHG allocation. The RFNBO reward does not multiply these physical quantities. Separate RFNBO banking and pooling are provided for in the regulatory framework, but are not implemented in this stress test. No equivalent-fuel exemption is assumed. GHG-intensity bank/pool balances cannot settle a separate RFNBO energy shortfall. Penalty totals include any assumed RFNBO charge, with both components separately available in exports.

Banking, pooling & borrowing

What to enter in FuelEU history

Banked surplus at case start is the ship's unused, verified GHG-intensity compliance surplus entering the first reporting year, in tCO₂e. Enter zero if none. Do not enter money, fuel tonnes, EU ETS allowances, the new case's expected surplus or an unresolved historical deficit. A 100 tCO₂e opening bank covering a 60 tCO₂e deficit leaves 40 tCO₂e to carry forward.

Consecutive penalty years before case start counts the uninterrupted reporting-year streak ending immediately before the first case year, after the previous years' settlements. It is not the number of all historical penalties or the amount paid. For a case starting in 2027, penalties in both 2025 and 2026 give 2; a penalty in 2025 followed by no penalty in 2026 gives 0. Payment in the following calendar year does not move the penalty's reporting year.

If another modelled deficit penalty occurs in the first case year, prior counts of 0, 1 and 2 give multipliers of 1.00, 1.10 and 1.20 respectively. A year without a modelled deficit penalty resets the streak. Previous penalty payments are not charged again. Both fields must be zero for a 2025 start; the count cannot exceed the reporting years since 2025. The app permits history only for ships above 5,000 GT.

The model uses one penalty-year counter for residual GHG-intensity deficits and any explicitly assumed-active RFNBO-subtarget shortfall. GHG bank balances do not settle that separate RFNBO shortfall. ETS costs and OPS port-call penalties are not inputs to this counter.

How banks and pools are evaluated

Opening banks belong to individual ships and first cover that ship’s deficit. Current surplus is carried forward unless contributed to a modelled fleet pool. This model reserves banked surplus for the originating ship. Commission guidance also permits offering banked surplus through pooling; that option is not evaluated here. This modelling restriction, together with the exclusion of subset pools and partial settlements, can overstate costs and affect strategy rankings. A pooled result is modelled only if the full included fleet can be settled with available current surplus and, where enabled, external capacity. Internal pools retain a positive buffer. External counterparties must retain a positive overall pool balance and meet Article 21 requirements; this is a condition of the estimate, not a verified fact. If capacity is insufficient, the model falls back to individual settlement and explains residual penalties.

Borrowing is not selected: it involves a ship-specific limit, 10% next-year repayment uplift, a ban on consecutive borrowing and restrictions on pooling. The ledger provides the numerical borrowing ceiling only. No terminal bank value, surplus sales, fuel availability expansion or automatic future rule changes are assumed.

Costs, prices & present-value comparison

Amounts are EUR, fuel is metric tonnes, electricity is MWh, energy is MJ, intensity is gCO₂e/MJ, and compliance balances and ETS emissions are tCO₂e. One MWh is 3,600 MJ; one tonne is 1,000,000 grams.

Present cost = Σ annual modelled cost ÷ (1 + discount rate)^(reporting year − first year)

The first reporting year is undiscounted. Annual cost includes all fuel purchases, electricity, ETS, residual FuelEU penalties, purchased external surplus, selected implementation costs and the entered common annual cost. The result is total modelled cost, not an ETS-only amount or full vessel operating cost.

  • Fuel and electricity prices follow the entered fuel-price escalation; the EUA price follows its own escalation.
  • The pool price, incremental annual equipment costs and common annual cost remain at their entered values. No separate escalation is inferred for them.
  • Fuel conversion, OPS and proposed wind capital costs are charged in full in the first year when selected. Existing-wing capital is sunk; its entered annual costs remain.
  • Annual consumption, technical performance and operating decisions remain fixed through the horizon. Regulatory targets, entered price escalation, banks and penalty streaks can change yearly.
  • No terminal bank value, surplus-sale revenue, borrowing strategy, tax, finance, hedge or charter-party allocation is inferred.

The report's price sensitivities reprice the saved strategies with EUA −20%, EUA +20%, pool price +20% and nominated alternative-fuel prices +20%. They retain the selected voyage-leg fuel fractions, shore-power and wind choices; they do not rerun the full optimization. The displayed winner is the lowest repriced saved strategy. Create a new case/run to optimize under different prices or performance.

Positive savings mean the reference present cost minus the evaluated strategy present cost. Compare alternative performance, fuel prices or investment dates as separate cases; a saved recommendation is not a forecast of future market prices.

Reports, audit trail & material limits

Start with the ranked strategies, then inspect what the selected strategy actually uses, the voyage-leg fuel replacements, annual FuelEU allocation and ship-by-ship settlement ledger. Policies with identical displayed prices can remain separate when their recorded operating choices or detailed costs differ. The lowest evaluated strategy is not a certified global optimum.

For wind cases, use What the package changes to reconcile the combined benefit; do not add independent wind, fuel and pooling savings. CSV exports expose annual costs, allocation and wind/leg details. The JSON audit retains the saved inputs, results, evidence references and engine/ruleset identifiers.

Saved reports are snapshots. Editing a case or updating the app does not recalculate an earlier report; create a new run to apply revised assumptions or methods. Older reports can use earlier allocation methods and editable complete WtW values. Review their recorded versions and factors before comparison.

The customer workflow covers oil, eligible non-food/feed HVO/FAME, LNG, bio-LNG and eligible RFNBO pathways for conventional cargo/tanker, container and passenger ships. Ordinary EU voyage percentages determine ETS emissions and the FuelEU covered energy total. FuelEU then attributes the annual energy sources separately using prioritized allocation. Ship-specific EEA application dates, small-island/outermost-region derogations, excluded port calls, transhipment ports, ice-class adjustments and other exemptions must be assessed separately. Port movements not included in a voyage need separate scope review: ETS covers these within-port emissions in full. The app has no separate port-movement category. A scope confirmation is required to run a case.

LNG, bio-LNG and e-LNG use the selected consumer's default slip: 3.1% Otto medium speed, 1.7% Otto slow speed, 0.2% Diesel slow speed or 2.6% LBSI. Combusted CO₂ and N₂O are reduced for the slipped fraction; methane is retained. Separate sea, berth and alternative methane consumer classes are modelled. Mixed engines, measured/load-dependent slip, additional venting and non-MGO pilot fuel for the alternative system need separate assessment. Mandatory OPS port-call penalties and exemptions for container/passenger ships are also outside the cost model. Consequently, the model cannot establish the full legal compliance cost for cases affected by those rules. HSFO requires a separately confirmed sulphur-compliance arrangement.

Prices and fuel properties are editable planning assumptions, not current market quotations or universal regulatory factors. Actual supplier batches, evidence validity, engine factors, operational fuel standards, fuel availability, costs under charter parties, taxes, financing, hedging and other regulations may change the commercial answer. Record additional common costs in the case assumptions and use the downloadable audit file for independent review.

Battery storage, onboard carbon capture, speed optimization, aerodynamic prediction and automated monitoring-plan or FuelEU database submissions are not evaluated features. The scope confirmation records the user's review; it does not perform a route-by-route legal assessment.
